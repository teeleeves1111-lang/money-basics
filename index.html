The 12-month raise rule
📈
When your pay goes up, keep your spending where it was for 12 months. Send the whole raise to savings and debt.
🧠
After a year you won't miss spending you never started, and the higher savings rate just feels normal. That's how Maya ends up saving a quarter of her pay without ever feeling a cut.
🚗
The first lifestyle upgrade is usually a car. A $600/month lease is $36,000 over five years with nothing to show for it. Invested instead, at 4% real, it's about $40,000.

Why it still feels wrong
You know someone who made a killing on Nvidia. So why doesn't the data match? Survivorship bias — you only hear about the winners.

The stocks you hear about are the survivors. Nobody posts about Peloton (−95%), Zoom (−85%), Rivian (−90%), or WeWork (bankrupt). The index fund owned Nvidia all the way up automatically — without guessing in advance, and without needing to hold through its 80% drawdown in 2022 without panic-selling.
The timing problem: Even picking the right stock isn't enough. Studies show most individual investors who owned Nvidia still underperformed the index — they sold too early or bought after the big run.
The 90/10 framework
We're not telling you to stop picking stocks. Instead, here's a structure that lets you play without putting the foundation at risk. It's about how you allocate each dollar, not which accounts you open.
The 90/10 rule — applied to every dollar you invest
🔒
Retirement accounts (Roth IRA, 401k) — 100% index funds, always. These are the vault. No stock picks, ever. FZROX + FZILX, or VTI + VXUS. Automated and untouched.
🟢
Taxable brokerage, 90 cents of every dollar — index funds. Same boring foundation continues here. This is the long-term engine inside your brokerage account.
🟡
Taxable brokerage, 10 cents of every dollar — your picks. This slice lives inside the same brokerage account. Pick stocks, try a sector ETF, whatever excites you. Real money, real consequences — that's how you learn.
💡
Key insight: The 10% slice could theoretically go to zero and it wouldn't sink you — because 90% of your taxable deposits plus 100% of your retirement accounts are still compounding. The account doesn't go to zero. A small slice of monthly contributions gets to be fun.

What to actually buy
Why not a target date fund?
Target date funds auto-shift from stocks to bonds as you approach retirement. That auto-pilot is useful at 55 — it costs you at 25. A 2065 fund today already holds ~10–15% in bonds, and keeps adding more every year. With a 40-year runway you can ride out any crash — time is your safety net. Bonds in your 20s just drag returns without giving you anything useful in return.
Simplest portfolio in your 20s: Two funds — FZROX + FZILX (Fidelity, 0% fee each) or VTI + VXUS (Vanguard/Fidelity/Schwab, 0.03–0.05%/yr). ~80% US, ~20% international. Rebalance once a year. Add bonds when you're within 15–20 years of retirement.

Job change fire drill
Changing jobs triggers five financial decisions with hard deadlines. Most people only negotiate salary — and forfeit thousands everywhere else.
The #1 mistake: Cashing out your old 401k. On a $20,000 balance you lose ~$6,000–7,000 to taxes and the 10% penalty — instantly, permanently. Roll it over instead.

Vesting schedules explained
Cliff vesting
0% vested until a specific date — then 100% instantly. Common: 1-year cliff. Leave one day early: forfeit everything.
Graded vesting
Vest gradually: e.g. 20%/yr over 5 years. Each year you leave earlier costs a specific slice. Check your plan documents.
Also check: RSU/stock option vesting dates, profit-sharing distributions, and whether your annual bonus has a "must be employed on payment date" clause. Timing your last day around these can be worth several months of salary.
You have 60 days from your last day of employer coverage to enroll in COBRA or an ACA marketplace plan. After that, you're uninsured until your new employer's plan starts.
FSA warning: If you have a Flexible Spending Account (FSA), it's use-it-or-lose-it. Spend down your balance before your last day — schedule dental, glasses, prescriptions. HSA funds are yours to keep regardless.

How to decide
Choose COBRA if…
You have ongoing care, prescriptions, or procedures already scheduled. Same doctors, same deductible mid-year. Worth the cost for continuity.
Choose marketplace if…
You're healthy, gap is short, or you want to check for a subsidy. Go to healthcare.gov — most young professionals qualify for meaningful subsidies. Special enrollment starts your last day; subsidies are income-based and often larger than people expect.
Tip: You can enroll in marketplace even if you haven't used COBRA yet. Job loss = qualifying life event = 60-day special enrollment period. You do not have to elect COBRA first.
